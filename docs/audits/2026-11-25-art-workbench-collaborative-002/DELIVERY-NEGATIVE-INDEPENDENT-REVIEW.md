## Timing scope and remaining uncertainty

Source review confirms the clock spans submit, rasterization, native admission,
receipt consumption and polling until main.image reports the changed material
pixel. It is not isolated native-worker duration or physical monitor latency.
The raster plan budgets 2,500 microseconds per slice, checks elapsed time every
64 candidate cells, and lets nonempty sparse buffers flush after a 33 ms age.
The executor advances rasterization at most once per process frame. These
mechanisms preserve bounded per-frame work but do not establish a universal
wall-time response bound under every frame schedule.

The printed 2,543 microsecond raster-slice observation belongs to an earlier
preparatory polygon, not the subsequently timed progress request. It cannot
localize the 2,187 ms interval. The record does not establish a provider, native,
renderer, OS-scheduling or host-contention cause. Earlier bcc and e536 progress
values are separate hosted observations and cannot supply that missing causal
evidence.

If a later investigation is needed, first-chunk, admission, receipt and image
publication timestamps plus frame gaps would separate these boundaries. That
is a diagnostic proposal, not a finding or an application change. The strict
two-second predicate remains intact.
