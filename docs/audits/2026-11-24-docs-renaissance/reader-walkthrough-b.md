## First-time user path

The root README gives the product overview and routes a new reader directly to
Getting started and the first Workbench session. The documentation portal offers
the same route under its audience-oriented entry points. The reader does not
need an old evaluator, a companion workspace or a chronological handover to find
the current launch and small authoring exercise.

| Reader task | Available direction | Source comparison and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Choose a usable installation | Source checkout, identified evaluator and incomplete archive are separate cases. | The checked Windows wrapper accepts Godot, Editor and identity options; it does not rebuild or forward arbitrary Godot flags. Source-only launch does not require `dev.cmd`. |
| Open the ordinary application | Feature launcher → **Development workbench**. | `feature_launcher.gd` and `project.godot` provide the route; standalone scenes are separately labelled. |
| Prepare a small exercise | Select Observer, keep the fresh scene paused, locate empty space and choose Paint. | `shared_workbench.gd` and the painting controller provide the actual labels, focus rules and paused tool path. |
| Paint and reverse a construction edit | Hard Wall floor/sides, Sand patch, then Undo/Redo while paused. | The native-backed painting controller/executor supplies those tools and receipts. The tutorial waits for completion instead of treating queue admission as success. |
| Save before simulation | Name and **Save start** the authored construction. | `save_setup` selects the authored writer. It does not capture the complete evolved World. |
| Observe material behavior | Play, observe and pause again; optional Water Lab comparison. | Current input paths and the fresh Workbench gameplay Water profile agree with the instructions. This source review does not qualify motion or playfeel. |
| Retain the intended state | Ordinary Save versus explicit Flatten followed by Save. | Flatten captures stored material IDs, discards previous construction/history and preserves the live World; it is not automatic disk Save or exact physical continuation. |
| Exit and reopen safely | Finish pending work, Menu/F2, reopen and Load start. | Menu closes the session. Authored layout reconstruction is separate from Undo history and session-only Art conversation. |
| Continue into richer authoring | Generator, painting/history, actors, Materials Lab, Water Lab and Art guides. | Preview/install, isolated Lab candidates and provider/native boundaries are explicit. Unmerged successors are not advertised as ordinary functionality. |

The small exercise uses built-in materials, no paid provider and no private map.
Its steps are practical source-backed instructions, not a claim that an owner
completed a fresh graphical acceptance run. The independent claim review is in
[review-b-by-a.json](review-b-by-a.json): all 43 B claims are independently
source-verified, with exact current document hashes and no open findings.
The later manual, standalone-host and sampled-actor scope corrections extend
that review; they do not recertify old physics or performance campaigns.

The final reading pass caught and corrected one small first-session ambiguity:
a newly opened Workbench is already paused, so Step 2 now says to keep it paused
and use Play / Pause only if it is running. A reread that final wording against
the same accepted source. This prevents the tutorial from accidentally starting
simulation before its precise construction exercise.

The final four-page World Art review found two additional operational defects:
the merged standalone pipeline still said Draft/HOLD, and its new-checkout
example selected the old integration branch. The accepted-main route and exact
Godot pin now lead those guides; original graphical results stay source-bound.

launchers and physical-acceptance script, then preserved byte-identically.
[The Art-tool review](late-world-art-review-b.json) records its reviewed scope
and limits, including that the frozen package was not downloaded or rerun.
