## Architectural assessment

These are informed, non-blinded model-specialist judgments. They are useful for the owner to assess the examples, not a preference experiment or acceptance vote.

| Composition | What the actual frames establish visually | Remaining architectural limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Orbital fabrication hangar, 1,024 × 1,024 | A tall central working bay is enclosed by unequal side galleries. A continuous roof truss, upper gantry, lower trussed work platform and side machinery give the empty volume a legible industrial purpose. Repeated detail is mostly attached to the larger framing and does not fill the central space indiscriminately. | Some side rooms and equipment are sparse; the suspended arm reads as a hoist-like element but has no demonstrated mechanical function. A static side elevation does not establish access to every gallery or physical load capacity. |
| Terraced arcology, 1,024 × 1,024 | Unequal tower heights, stepped roofs, a central vertical shaft and secondary bridges create a different silhouette from the hangar. Large horizontal floor bands remain legible, and the continuous central pier provides visible framing continuity. | Repeated window assemblies and mostly empty rectangular rooms still dominate. The shaft and occasional stairs do not demonstrate a complete inter-floor circulation network. Windows and detached treads/details are material features, not proof that rooms are inhabited, reachable or mutually connected. |
| Monumental viaduct, 1,536 × 768 | Four arched openings, substantial piers and a continuous upper deck create the clearest repeated structural rhythm. The long lower gallery and differently placed braces, trusses and service bays give useful hierarchy below the deck. Fine surface marks remain subordinate to the arches. | The geometric massing reads as construction, but no engineering load analysis is implied. Lower service spaces and stairs require route-specific assessment; a single bounded actor path cannot certify the entire vertical network. |
| Biomechanical vault, 1,024 × 768 | An irregular rock envelope surrounds a broad constructed arch and clear central floor. Side service frames and branch-like assemblies distinguish the composition from the regular towers and viaduct. The arch is continuous and visually dominant over detail. | The central volume is intentionally very open and the side installations remain schematic. Branch-like material assemblies do not establish ecological, biological, fluid or machine behavior. A biomechanical theme is an architectural interpretation, not a simulated property. |

All whole views preserve true orthographic side elevation. No isometric top faces, faux perspective or visual-only geometry substitutes were observed. The upper and lower district frames are predetermined central crops: they make the trusses, arch edges and shaft readable at their native scale, but often devote much of the image to the intended central void. They are not exhaustive coverage of every side district or circulation transition.

No newly observed visual defect justified changing geometry during this freeze. The remaining sparseness, repetition and incomplete access evidence are retained limitations, not silently relabeled successes. Material-component counts, declared clearance checks, native receipt replay, actual actor trajectories and visual judgments remain separate evidence. Physical owner acceptance, overall architectural preference and real-model planning remain pending.
