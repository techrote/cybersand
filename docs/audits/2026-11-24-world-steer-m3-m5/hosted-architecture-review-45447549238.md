## Cross-platform rendering and limits

Every final native material plane matches exactly across Linux, Windows source, and extracted Windows. All nine Windows source PNG files also match their extracted-evaluator counterparts exactly. Linux used `llvmpipe` with OpenGL; Windows used ANGLE with the Microsoft Basic Render Driver. All three bound the same palette source hash, `f686ca416b5b011751ddd5d38a24824742d01cb8d489b46710ee1f1fbb2a09c1`.

Linux and Windows PNGs are **not** byte-identical. Direct RGBA comparison finds many small channel differences and, depending on the frame, 48–324 pixels with a maximum per-channel difference greater than four. Individual larger differences occur in the textured materials. The companion records exact counts and maxima. Viewing all eighteen Linux and Windows source frames found matching architectural silhouettes, scale, contact geometry, and framing; this is a visual observation alongside exact native equality, not a claim of cross-platform pixel equivalence. Renderer precision or color behavior was not separately isolated.

Single-component material masks establish static contact. They do not establish structural strength, physics stability, material lifetime, fluid flow, actor clearance, jump reach, or complete gameplay traversal. Declared empty rectangles are specific geometric contracts, not evidence that every intended route is reachable. This review does not infer model quality, retrieval quality, blind human preference, or owner acceptance from these authored examples. The thirty-attempt matched comparison remains a separate evidence cohort with its own frozen registration and limited conclusions.

The separate interactive viewport framing defect discovered after this campaign is retained as a genuine product defect despite successful automated checks. It does not alter the native or nine-frame findings above. Later qualification must bind its UI correction and final evaluator to their own exact source and artifact identities.
