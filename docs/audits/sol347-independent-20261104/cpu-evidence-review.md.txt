## Confirmed cost result and immediate classification

There are 24 groups (2 OS × 2 configured workers × 3 environments × 2 arms), each three repeats. Using the largest of each group's individual case means, 23/24 groups pass the unchanged 2 ms criterion; all 12 candidate groups pass. The only failure is Windows, four workers, embedded-Sand baseline r0. It is not a candidate-wide CPU failure and must not be removed as an outlier.

Its original metric is `(all owner observation+metadata+freezing elapsed + all main Sand-response elapsed) / complete consumed geometry observations`:

`(480.685 ms + 0.000 ms) / 224 = 2.145915178571 ms`, 7.295759% above 2 ms.

An exact algebraic diagnostic factorization is `1.302669376694 ms per owner attempt × 369/224 attempts per complete observation = 2.145915178571`. This is an explanation of the preserved metric, not a replacement denominator. No Sand-response call occurred in that baseline; neither accepted nor refused Sand-response work causes this failed numerator. The original conservative-displacement and ordinary cellular coupling still run in the baseline.

r0's 645,867 geometry probes and 1,352,007 support probes show a substantially heavier realized owner workload; native sampling performs nine support-residency checks for each qualifying overlapping ambient-Sand cell before constructing exterior-face contact dictionaries. At r0's last observation the aggregate overlaps 469.162326 cell² of Sand, versus 176.684177 and 131.087910 in r1/r2. These are unmatched physical trajectories. The differences support workload dependence; they do not establish why one trajectory buried further or what portion of time came from contention.

Producer/consumer amplification is directly visible: the r0 trace retains 224 observations spanning native ticks 2–360 and snapshots 11–369; consecutive native/snapshot gaps are 1 on 88 occasions and 2 on 135 occasions. There are 135 unretained publication slots inside that observed span, plus setup/leading work in the cumulative numerator. This is latest-value asynchronous consumption, not proof of 135 missed simulation steps or a broken queue. The 145 attempt-minus-complete difference must not all be labelled overwritten publications without their outcomes.

**Causal classification:** confirmed owner contact-observation workload/consumption-cost overrun in the baseline control; high realized Sand-support/contact work and low consumption efficiency are measured contributors. Pure geometry algorithm cost versus Dictionary construction/freezing versus scheduling preemption is not identified. A four-worker contention defect is unproven. Contact observation itself is a serial exclusive-owner callback, with no pool-parallel work in its loops. Configured workers=4 is not measured four-way participation; participation telemetry is not retained in this contact report.

## What the existing timers do and do not separate

| Timer/data | Actual scope | Interpretation limit |
|---|---|---|
| Native `observation_ms` | `steady_clock` over the full native adapter callback, including identity checks, rectangle/exterior-span reconstruction, cell queries/SAT/clipping/support, Dictionary/Array construction and final metrics | It is not pure geometry arithmetic or CPU-thread time; only consumed successful samples are summed in `geometry_ms_total`. |
| `worker_contact_ms_total` | Cumulative owner wall time over every native observation call plus metadata attachment and recursive contact freezing | Includes refused/unconsumed/setup work up to last consumption; ends before actual publication mutex assignment. There is no cumulative all-attempt native subtotal or separate freeze subtotal. |
| `response_ms_all_attempts` | Outer main-thread wall time for every Sand-response call, including identity preflight, accepted or refused planning, RID operations and readback | Accepted `response_ms_total` is a narrower inner interval and cannot replace this numerator. |
| `main_sync_ms_total` | Consumption/ordinary coupling/Sand response plus compound-state publication, two measured segments per Rapier step | Nests Sand response; do not add it to the existing cost numerator or call it isolated lock waiting. |
| `observed_worker_step_ms_total` / `observed_native_tick_ms_total` | Consumed snapshots' normal owner step and nested native simulation time | Different populations and timers from contact work; owner step is timed before `_publish_snapshot`, hence before contact sampling. Not all native ticks or all worker CPU. |
| `rapier_step_ms_total` | Whole bridge `step()`: backend manual step, active transforms, callback flush and dynamic-state refresh | Distinct from main response and owner contact sampling; no collision-only subtotal in these reports. |
| `published_usec`, response latency | Contact timestamp is set after the native callback but before freezing/publication; accepted-response latency is sampled after planning, impulses and readback | End-to-end age, not isolated publication wait; baseline zero latency total means no accepted responses, not zero observation latency. Refused/non-attempted age is missing. |

For the failed baseline, native-observation elapsed summed only over 224 consumed observations is 209.3049 ms (0.934396875 ms/complete). Subtracting it from 480.685 yields 271.3801 ms containing both all unconsumed native work and metadata/freezing/overhead. Calling that difference a freeze cost would be invalid.

Separate r0 costs: Rapier bridge 68.308 ms / 360 steps = 0.189744444 ms/step; main synchronization 40.230 ms / 720 segments = 0.055875 ms/segment (two segments/step); observed native 67.9881 ms / 224 snapshots = 0.303518304 ms; observed worker 140.765 ms / 224 = 0.628415179 ms. These do not enter the registered 2 ms contact-work numerator. Both native and owner timers are monotonic elapsed-time proxies; operating-system preemption can inflate them. There is no thread CPU/idle/lock trace to prove scheduling contention.

## Physical results, stronger endpoint evidence and scope

Independently reproduced maxima are packed candidate 4.04673163998175 cells (Linux, four workers, r2), embedded candidate 2.64279245542222 cells (Linux, four workers, r0), and positive sampled spans 5.933745–5.984297 seconds. These are SAT per-square/proxy minimum separating depths at copied publication poses, not aggregate COM burial depths or between-step sweep penetration.

All 36 candidate runs still have positive Sand overlap at their last retained observation, corresponding to Rapier step 359 or 360. Final observed maximum Sand depth ranges 2.06773082286146–3.82840517343631 cells and final Sand area 8.56134990662176–64.7206886850061 cell². There are 12,077 positive candidate observations among 12,087; the ten nonpositive observations are the first record of their respective cases. This is strong sampled endpoint evidence against a claim of clearance during the tested interval; it remains insufficient to prove continuous overlap between all unobserved steps.

The environment named `embedded_sand` authors the bank around native solids, skipping every nonempty cell. It does not stamp Sand into the initial RedBrick payload. Embedded uses 2,016 Sand cells, packed uses 1,176; both Water mass zero. Across all candidate runs, the first retained depth is 0–0.0159912109375 cells. Multi-cell overlap is therefore acquired during the recorded moving session, rather than explained solely by the name of the initial fixture. A replay that starts at a deeply buried sampled pose must explicitly identify that as its replay initial state.

The test observes native publications asynchronously after each actual Rapier step. It does not record every native step, full contact dictionaries, all native cell tuples, per-step body linear velocities, per-response outcomes/impulses, or post-impulse energies. Historical trace data alone cannot deterministically reconstruct the full physical state or attach each peak-depth event to its response disposition. A new frozen capture is necessary for synchronized replay.

No general live maximum-depth or clearance-duration number was preregistered. The original >=25% controlled added-overlap improvement and energy gates can pass while sustained gameplay clearance remains unqualified. Do not retroactively turn 4.05 or 2.64 cells into a numerical failure criterion that never existed, and do not treat that absence as physical acceptance.

`Accepted` includes zero-impulse plans when there are no approaching contacts. The 10,721 accepts must not be described as 10,721 physical pushes. Each of the 36 candidate cases has a positive total impulse sum, proving some real response, but the archive cannot split accepted-no-op versus accepted-mutating applications. There are 42 source observations with zero contact records; no event-level join proves their final response categories.

`NonDissipativeResponse` is a pure-planner rejection before RID writes. It is distinct from actual backend energy growth, of which none is recorded. Its 332 events, including 89 embedded, justify retaining the exact rejected value inputs for diagnosis. The existing trace does not determine whether a rejected planned step is numerical precision, an interacting contact/rotation case or another reason; it must not be fixed by relaxing energy tolerance without evidence.

Deep overlap can coexist with few or no contact records because the observer only admits supported ambient Sand crossing actual exterior edges; interior Sand squares contribute overlap metrics but no exterior face segment. The velocity-only dissipative response cannot by itself remove pre-existing depth when no approaching motion remains. Four fixed sweeps and grouped resultant contacts also do not prove every local approach speed is eliminated. These are plausible/contact-law limitations from source, not a source-matched causal verdict on every live failure.
