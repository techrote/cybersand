## Synchronized replay and apparatus correction

The new executable replay retains the original native 520-cell payload and six
proxies, then holds the native material field fixed while the actual Rapier
bridge advances 180 declared 1/60-second steps. Seven fixtures cover stationary
burial, packed impact, off-centre impact, rotated geometry, missed delivery,
one-step delay and stale two-step delivery. Each runs with no extra Sand
response, the byte-frozen `749fb8b5` planner and the repaired planner.

This frozen-field baseline is a causal control for the selected response. It is
not the ordinary asynchronous conservative-displacement/GCR session, which
remains separately measured. Every physics step has exact rotated proxy
geometry, source/current poses, both hole witnesses and overlap measurements,
even when response delivery is deliberately missed. Only these complete traces
use cell-ticks. Live asynchronous reports retain cell-samples and area-samples.
Identical frozen observations are also offered to both planners at each receipt;
subsequent divergent body histories are not called identical inputs.

Author review, independently confirmed, found that the first development replay allowed Rapier sleep.
Some baseline/stale bodies stopped at step 31 with kinetic energy dropping from
2340 to zero without contact response. Candidate impulses could keep bodies
awake. The original reports therefore **fail apparatus comparability despite
successful test execution**. They are retained rather than used to claim contact
resistance. The corrected fixture disables sleep identically in all three arms
and records sleeping state and energy change across each Rapier step. This is
an explicit common-control correction, not a new production law or retrospective
change to the registered penetration/clearance limits.

## CPU failure, optimization and accounting

The old Windows/four-worker embedded baseline r0 remains a registered failure:
480.685 ms of all owner observation/freezing work, 369 owner attempts and 224
complete consumed observations give **2.145915178571 ms per observation**.
Main response work is zero. Per-attempt owner cost is 1.302669 ms. The 145-attempt
difference includes setup and unconsumed attempts; it is not falsely labelled
145 proven overwrites. The consumed native geometry totals 209.3049 ms, with
maximum 1.8863 ms per sample. Sustained costly geometry and fewer complete
receipts amplify the inclusive mean; this evidence does not isolate an operating
system scheduler event or a single anomalous pause.

The native optimization seals scalar-only contact/proxy dictionaries and their
arrays at construction. The worker seals its metadata envelope, with the old
recursive freezer retained for markerless runtime compatibility. Observation-
constant exterior endpoints and normals move outside the per-cell loop without
changing arithmetic or iteration order. No observation-rate reduction, attempt
exclusion, cross-sample cache or denominator change is admitted.

Forty identical native queries produce bit-for-bit equal semantic JSON before
and after optimization after excluding timing and immutability metadata,
including the pre-existing `observation_ms` timer.
The new observer suite verifies immutable records, legacy fallback, complete and
refused attempts, partial seams and float32 coherence with 142 checks per worker
configuration. All-attempt native-call and envelope intervals sum to the original
inclusive owner interval; native geometry/freezing are nested stage measurements
and are not counted twice. Every main response attempt remains in the numerator.
The denominator remains complete consumed observations, and each of the original
24 groups uses its largest mean across three repeats against the unchanged 2 ms
limit. Missing cohorts produce `INCOMPLETE`, not a pass.

These are elapsed monotonic intervals around relevant work, as in the registered
historical gate. They are not operating-system thread CPU counters. Queue/receipt
latency is reported separately, and asynchronous histories are descriptive ranges
with sample variance rather than matched causal speedups.

The new local Linux development cohorts preserve a further failed observation:
worker 1, embedded candidate r2 accumulates 116.446 ms of owner work across 369
attempts and 408.867 ms across 245 main response attempts. Dividing their sum by

is `INCOMPLETE`. This result is retained regardless of subsequent hosted runs.
The remaining cost in this case is mainly main-thread response evaluation, not
the optimized owner envelope. No claim that optimization universally meets the
cost gate follows from the lower baseline or matched output equality.

The [local raw reports and complete analysis](sol364-20261104/development-cost/manifest.json)
